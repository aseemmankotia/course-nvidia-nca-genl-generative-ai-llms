## Chapter 6: Experimentation and Evaluation — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Perplexity score: higher or lower is better for language models? | LOWER is better. Perplexity = exp(cross-entropy loss). Measures how 'surprised' the model is by the data. Lower perplexity = better next-token prediction. Exam tip: Commonly reversed - remember lower = less perplexed = better. |
| 2 | BLEU vs ROUGE: which for translation, which for summarization? | BLEU = translation/generation (precision-focused, n-gram overlap). ROUGE = summarization (recall-focused, measures how much reference content is captured). Exam tip: BLEU-T (Translation), ROUGE-R (Recall for summaries). |
| 3 | What does p-value < 0.05 mean in A/B testing? | Statistical significance - less than 5% chance results are due to random chance. Does NOT mean: (1) practical significance, (2) large effect size, or (3) ready to deploy. Exam tip: Statistical ≠ practical significance. |
| 4 | Ablation study: what is it measuring? | Impact of individual components by removing them one at a time. Measures performance drop when component is absent. Larger drop = more important component. Exam tip: Used to justify architectural choices and identify critical components. |
| 5 | ROUGE-1 vs ROUGE-2 vs ROUGE-L: what's the difference? | ROUGE-1 = unigram overlap (individual words). ROUGE-2 = bigram overlap (two-word phrases). ROUGE-L = longest common subsequence. Exam tip: Higher number = longer n-grams; L = sequence order matters. |
| 6 | LLM-as-judge: main advantage and main risk? | Advantage: scales evaluation of open-ended generation where BLEU/ROUGE fail. Risk: systematic bias toward outputs similar to judge's training style. Exam tip: Still needs periodic human evaluation for validation. |
| 7 | MMLU benchmark: what does it test? | Massive Multitask Language Understanding - 57 subjects from STEM to humanities. Tests broad knowledge coverage. Range: 0-100%. Exam tip: High MMLU = strong general knowledge, but doesn't guarantee task-specific performance. |
| 8 | Control vs treatment in A/B testing: which is which? | Control = baseline/existing version. Treatment = new version being tested. Split traffic between both, measure performance difference. Exam tip: Always need a control group to measure relative improvement. |
| 9 | Perplexity calculation formula? | Perplexity = exp(cross-entropy loss). Or: exp(-1/N × Σ log P(word\|context)). Exam tip: Don't need to calculate by hand - just know lower = better and it's exponential of loss. |
| 10 | BLEU score range and what 1.0 means? | Range: 0.0 to 1.0. Score of 1.0 = perfect n-gram match with reference (rarely achieved). Typical good scores: 0.3-0.6 depending on task. Exam tip: Don't expect scores near 1.0 even for good systems. |
| 11 | What's the difference between GLUE and SuperGLUE benchmarks? | GLUE = 9 language understanding tasks (sentiment, entailment, etc.). SuperGLUE = harder successor with more challenging tasks. Both test general language understanding. Exam tip: SuperGLUE was created because models were saturating GLUE. |
| 12 | Human evaluation scales: typical rating dimensions? | Common dimensions: Fluency (grammar/readability), Coherence (logical flow), Relevance (answers the question), Factuality (accurate information). Usually 1-5 or 1-7 scale. Exam tip: More expensive but gold standard for open-ended tasks. |
