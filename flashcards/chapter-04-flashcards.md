## Chapter 4: Alignment, Safety and Responsible AI — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the three phases of RLHF training in order? | 1) Supervised fine-tuning (instruction tuning), 2) Reward model training on human preference data, 3) RL optimization (PPO) using the reward model. Exam tip: Reward model must exist BEFORE RL phase can begin. |
| 2 | Hallucination definition in LLMs? | Model generating plausible but factually incorrect or unverifiable information. Exam tip: Hallucinations can occur with HIGH confidence scores because models predict likely tokens, not truth. |
| 3 | Primary technique to mitigate hallucination in production systems? | Grounding via RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): retrieve verified documents and instruct model to base responses on retrieved content with citations. Exam tip: Temperature reduction helps but doesn't eliminate hallucinations. |
| 4 | Prompt injection vs jailbreak attack? | Prompt injection: malicious instructions in user input to override system prompt. Jailbreak: bypassing safety guardrails (e.g., 'pretend you're DAN' attacks). Exam tip: Both exploit the model's inability to distinguish trusted vs untrusted input. |
| 5 | Most effective defense against prompt injection attacks? | Separate safety classifier evaluating semantic intent, operating outside LLM context. Exam tip: Keyword filters are easily bypassed; system prompts can be overridden; classifiers provide architectural separation. |
| 6 | Purpose of instruction tuning (supervised fine-tuning)? | Transform base completion model into instruction-following assistant. Teaches format and behavior for responding to user commands/questions. Exam tip: Happens BEFORE RLHF reward model training. |
| 7 | What does the reward model in RLHF predict? | Human preference score for model outputs (which response humans prefer). Trained on comparison data where humans rank multiple outputs. Exam tip: Reward model is separate from the policy model being trained. |
| 8 | Guardrails in AI safety: three main types? | 1) Input filtering (block malicious prompts), 2) Output filtering (toxicity/safety classifiers), 3) Structural constraints (RAG grounding, allowed actions). Exam tip: Layered defense - use multiple types together. |
| 9 | Why can't prompt engineering alone eliminate bias/toxicity? | Models may follow malicious instructions, prompts can be overridden, and models can't always distinguish intent. Need architectural safeguards (classifiers, filters) + training-time intervention (RLHF). Exam tip: Prompts are necessary but not sufficient. |
| 10 | Relationship between temperature and hallucination? | Lower temperature (more deterministic) reduces randomness but doesn't eliminate hallucination. Temperature 0.0 still hallucinates if highest-probability token is factually wrong. Exam tip: Temperature controls randomness, not truthfulness. |
| 11 | Citation verification in RAG systems - why critical? | Models can hallucinate citations even when told to cite sources. Verify citations exist in retrieval database post-generation. Exam tip: LLM can fabricate plausible-looking references; always validate programmatically. |
| 12 | Alignment in AI: core definition? | Ensuring model behavior matches human intent and values. Achieved through: instruction tuning, RLHF, safety filters, and ongoing monitoring. Exam tip: Alignment is process, not one-time fix; requires multiple techniques. |
